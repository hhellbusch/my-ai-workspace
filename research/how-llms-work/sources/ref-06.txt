# How do Transformers work?
Source: https://huggingface.co/learn/llm-course/chapter1/4
Fetched: 2026-09-02 (WebFetch)

The Transformer architecture was introduced in June 2017. The focus of the original research was on translation tasks. Influential models after that include GPT (June 2018), BERT (October 2018), GPT-2 (February 2019), T5 (October 2019), GPT-3 (May 2020), InstructGPT (January 2022), Llama, Mistral, Gemma 2, SmolLM2.

Broadly grouped into three categories:
- GPT-like (auto-regressive Transformer models)
- BERT-like (auto-encoding Transformer models)
- T5-like (sequence-to-sequence Transformer models)

All the Transformer models mentioned above have been trained as language models. This means they have been trained on large amounts of raw text in a self-supervised fashion. Self-supervised learning is a type of training in which the objective is automatically computed from the inputs of the model. Humans are not needed to label the data.

This type of model develops a statistical understanding of the language it has been trained on, but it's less useful for specific practical tasks. Because of this, the general pretrained model then goes through transfer learning or fine-tuning — additional training with a dataset specific to a task.

An example of a task is predicting the next word in a sentence having read the n previous words. This is called causal language modeling because the output depends on the past and present inputs, but not the future ones. Another example is masked language modeling.

The general strategy to achieve better performance is by increasing the models' sizes as well as the amount of data they are pretrained on. Sharing trained weights reduces compute cost.

Pretraining is training from scratch: weights randomly initialized, large corpus, can take weeks. Fine-tuning is additional training after pretraining, on a task-specific dataset, with much less data/time/compute.

## General Transformer architecture

- Encoder: receives an input and builds a representation of it (features). Optimized to acquire understanding from the input.
- Decoder: uses the encoder's representation along with other inputs to generate a target sequence. Optimized for generating outputs.

Each part can be used independently:
- Encoder-only: sentence classification, NER
- Decoder-only: text generation
- Encoder-decoder / seq2seq: translation, summarization

## Attention layers

Attention layers tell the model to pay specific attention to certain words in the sentence (and more or less ignore the others) when dealing with the representation of each word. A word by itself has a meaning, but that meaning is deeply affected by the context.

## The original architecture

Designed for translation. Encoder attention can use all words in a sentence. Decoder works sequentially and can only pay attention to words already translated. During training the decoder is fed the whole target but is not allowed to use future words (causal mask).

The first attention layer in a decoder block pays attention to all (past) inputs to the decoder; the second attention layer uses the output of the encoder.

## Architectures vs. checkpoints

- Architecture: skeleton of the model
- Checkpoints: the weights loaded into an architecture
- Model: umbrella term for both
